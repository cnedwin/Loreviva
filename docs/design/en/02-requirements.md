## 02 Product Objectives and Scope

The creator flow is template/idea → world and characters → stage → role Skills → rehearsal and publication → instance. The player flow is entry and interaction → consequences → memory and relationships → pause and return → material controls. The customer flow is identity → industry package → interaction and recovery → costs and entitlements. Pages and acceptance are organized around these three flows.

The first unit is a rainy-night café, two actors, one main respondent, wooden-sign choices, private disclosure, promises, and return, using a browser or chat client. The core covers multiple worlds, stable personality, long-term experiences, refusal and disableable surprises, a 2D/2.5D stage, integration, and metering. Full 3D, MMO, VR, free responses by multiple actors, a commercial marketplace, and automatic installation are deferred and require dedicated budgets and acceptance.

## 03 Users, Responsibilities, and Permissions

The server authorizes by tenant, product, project, instance, object, and action. Responsibilities may be combined and are not granted by AI, packages, or clients. Authorized people may hold editing, checking, and publishing responsibilities; two-person approval is not mandatory. Administration rights do not automatically expose private body content. Finance sees only necessary references and amounts.

| Responsibility preset | Permitted behavior | Key restriction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer | View approved drafts, assets, and authorized reports | Cannot change content or read private chats without permission |
| Creator/editor | Create, copy, and edit drafts, import material, initiate approved rehearsals | Cannot publish or upgrade instances without publishing rights |
| Publisher | Check publication candidates, fix versions, approve content upgrades | Cannot bypass missing rights, models, budgets, or required acceptance |
| Runtime manager | Start, pause, resume, end; inspect tasks and recovery status | Cannot rewrite established events or confirm choices for players |
| Memory governor | Correct, revoke, delete, and inspect cleanup status within authorization | Operations identity does not provide all body content; cannot change relationships without provenance |
| Financial manager | Price lists, bills, reconciliation, authorized adjustments | Cannot delete posting; risk handling requires separate authorization |
| Workspace administrator | Manage member permissions, model connections, product integration, quotas | Keys are server-side references only; cannot broaden authority across tenants |
| Platform risk role | Handle UNKNOWN cases, verification, and risk-adjustment tickets | Must have scope, reason, evidence, and authoritative accounting records |
| Customer service identity | Exchange sessions, manage this product's instances, provide business receipts | Access only its own scope; SDK cannot write balances or facts |
| Player | Approved interactions, pause and leave, manage own memory and permissions | Global control of a shared world requires separate eligibility |
| Agent | Read task-approved material, call allowlisted tools, propose candidates | No direct fact, accounting, privilege-escalation, publication, or installation rights |

Authorization distinguishes unauthenticated, object invisible, and visible but inoperable. Invisible objects receive a uniform invisible response. Names, counts, logs, and resource URLs must not reveal their existence. In a shared world, “member leaving” and “pausing the whole world” require different permissions. Every external send, commit, result read, and resource read rechecks current permission. A fixed release version cannot override revocation, deletion, disabling, or hard budget limits.

Studio uses an independent OperatorBearer and scoped runtime.create/read/control actions. Administrative instance creation, listing, detail, control, and safe actor-metadata interfaces call the same domain services. Customer ServiceBearer does not enter the browser; being a Studio administrator does not grant player private data. Instance 202 control returns an actual administrative task whose established status is queried. Creation/start may be chosen after publication; they cannot occur automatically together. Actor-template/initial-setting editing is separate from running actor state: long-term personality changes use explicit content migration, while memory and relationships change only through approved source governance and runtime commits.

## 04 Objects and Lifecycles

REQ01-01: A world project holds world definitions, drafts, rehearsals, and releases. A world definition includes background, scenes, hard and soft rules, initial characters, roles, Skills, knowledge, assets, and an industry product package. A release fixes valid content and dependencies. A world instance references one version and carries participants, current facts, events, clock, controls, and actual experiences. A project may have multiple releases and instances. Saving an edited draft cannot update online state.

REQ01-02: A character definition saves personality, goals, expression, appearance and voice, initial knowledge, and relationship strategy. An actor instance references character and world versions and holds position, current goals, emotion, plans, learned knowledge, and experiences. Copying an actor instance into content extracts only approved static settings, without player memory. A relationship binds at least tenant, product, world instance, actor instance, and player; actor-to-actor relationships also have direction and provenance. A business user ID cannot be a unified cross-product profiling key.

REQ01-03: Every reference has a local ID, owning scope, fixed version, provenance, and availability state. World-definition states are draft and archived; release states are immutable, disabled, or governance-restricted; instance states are created, running, paused, and ended. Creation does not automatically start, and ending does not delete material. Long-term personality changes, application upgrades, database migrations, and instance content upgrades are separate operations.

R0 local settings and members: reuse the sole registered credential/current bearer identity, real TENANT and PRODUCT authorization/expiry, and current precise eligibility for existing content actions. Permissions granted to members must be limited to the subset the issuer can actually delegate under its current authority. Fixed CLI binds credentials to registered subjects in the same tenant and explicitly initializes the original owner on an old installation; ordinary Open/restore does not reseed. A command reducing maintenance entry points requires the currently authenticated caller to retain a non-expired TENANT maintenance.manage after commit. Handoff requires the replacement to actually log in before revoking the old holder. Self private generation INITIAL/RENEW/REVOKE additionally checks the original credential/independent action/current source/CAS/original key, with at most DB now+30 days; it cannot revive a deleted source. No SSO/dual-token UI/free SQL/secret readback. Local settings ports, the sole machine source, and migration must agree.

R0 Studio provides five navigation groups, normal properties/SVG tree, independent save/cancel, and 7 settings-command interfaces with a 25-type increment. It requires actual TENANT/PRODUCT permission, current multiple credentials, own 30-day INITIAL/RENEW/REVOKE, persistent connection disabling, original-request recovery, and local-display-only notifications. Skill category labels grant no execution rights; the wire has no per-binding parameter map. Four-format import, customer SDK, maintenance, and human acceptance retain their respective owning ports and acceptance responsibilities.

Common fields for every creation object are ID, name, type, description, tags, owning scope, status, draft revision, author/modification time, provenance and license, dependencies/references, and verification result. Field constraints: name is required, 1 through 80 characters after trimming whitespace; description is at most 2000 characters; at most 20 tags, each 1 through 32 characters, deduplicated within an object. System IDs and audit times are read-only. Extension properties must declare a Schema and errors locate the field. Free text cannot replace audience, version, amount, or permission.

REQ09-01: Every generation module has an “AI assistance” entry and a “model” field: inherited/specified, connection, model identifier, parameters, capability check, resolved source, current actual configuration, budget, and external-send scope. World, character, role, generative Skill, knowledge distillation, styling/voice, rehearsal, and offline review may each be configured. Programmatic Skills show “does not call a model” and tool capabilities. Normal forms remain available; unavailable models do not prevent editing that does not depend on AI.

Empty states offer templates/creation/import prerequisites. Permission messages do not reveal hidden content; failed loading preserves the user's place. No results and no reading eligibility are separate; counts do not disclose other scopes. Unknown amounts show pending verification; rates with a zero denominator cannot be calculated.

## 06 Unified Object Operations and Reference Effects

REQ07-01: Creation generates only a draft or unstarted instance and saves after required-field validation. Creating from a template first shows license, dependencies, required capabilities, and Mock mode. Identical names do not identify the same object. Before copying, show the copy list, shared references, and remapping. Users choose to retain fixed shared dependencies or independently copy approved content. Credentials, player identities, private chats, private memory, relationships, runtime facts, tasks, idempotency keys, orders, bills, and approval status are never copied.

REQ07-02: Editing shows its base revision. Adopting an AI candidate writes only to the local editing area, showing old value, new value, provenance, trust boundary, affected references, and estimated costs per field. Users may adopt one item, all valid items, or withdraw unsaved adoption. The program validates saving and creates a new draft revision; cancellation discards unsaved fields, and saved versions may separately be restored. Closing a page warns about unsaved changes. Canceling the interface does not cancel an accepted model task; stopping requires an independent cancellation command.

REQ07-03: Concurrent saves or outdated AI-candidate baselines preserve local fields and show three-way differences and conflicts without silent overwrite. Nonconflicting fields may be reapplied; conflicting fields are chosen individually and validated again. AI recomputation is a new bounded creation task and must show costs. Restoring a draft also creates a new revision; it does not erase historical releases, rehearsal events, or accounting.

| Object | Archive/disable effect | Deletion and reference handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World/character definition | Stops new adoption; previous releases continue with fixed dependencies | Unpublished and unreferenced content may be deleted; content referenced by releases/instances is archived first and historical dependencies cannot be dismantled |
| Scene/rule/role binding | Detaches in draft; publication requires rechecking | Locate referencing rules/actors/assets and save after repair; running instances do not change automatically |
| Skill/role preset | Stops adoption by new tasks or releases; governance disabling takes effect immediately | Fixed dependencies cannot dangle; replace referenced drafts first; deletion/disabling does not undo executed external actions |
| Knowledge/assets | Ordinary archiving stops new adoption | Rights revocation or privacy deletion uses governance, propagating to summaries, indexes, assets, outputs, and restricted historical access |
| Model connection | Stops new external sends; in-flight attempts remain queryable and reconcilable | Disable first when referenced by releases; explicitly verify replacement routing; do not delete unknown-cost records |
| World/actor instance | Pause or end retains events and material-control entry points | Deletion is a scoped governance task: block access before cleanup; retain minimum legally required accounting metadata |
| Bill/posting/order | Closing, reversal, and refund each have states | Ordinary deletion is prohibited; adjustments append references to original entries; historical costs are not erased |

REQ07-04: Confirmation panels for significant operations list object, scope, immediate/future effects, irreversible parts, in-flight tasks, and costs that may continue. Ordinary archiving differs from privacy deletion. Save, cancel, rehearse, publish, instance upgrade, disable, revoke, delete, and rollback each have independent receipts. Users cannot infer another action has completed from a button name.

REQ07-08: Creation uses typed aggregate drafts: WORLD, SCENE, PROP, RULE, CHARACTER, ROLE, SKILL, KNOWLEDGE, ASSET, PRESENTATION_THEME. Common metadata and each type's content are validated separately; properties bind a Schema. The server generates system ID, scope, author, hash, revision, and check state. Creating WORLD without a specified project creates project and draft in the same controlled commit. Independent assets may have no project but must have tenant/product boundaries. Drafts are DRAFT, ARCHIVED, or ISOLATED_IMPORT. Publishing separately creates an immutable release; it does not turn the draft into PUBLISHED.

REQ07-09: Saving uses expected_draft_revision. The same idempotency key and same normalized parameters first query the original commit, rather than returning a new conflict because an old revision already completed. Static impact/publication checks synchronously return a report bound to fixed revision and hash. Publication rechecks current rights, dependencies, capabilities, responsibilities, and validity; success returns release with 201. Long rehearsals, candidates, imports, or extractions return a product-scoped administrative task with 202. Closing the interface does not cancel it. Drafts, release, administrative tasks, and files are read under current identity and owning scope.

## 07 Editing Worlds, Stages, and Rules

World properties include name, background, language, industry package, private/shared form, initial scene, character list, participant types, default model, budget mode, memory retention and carryover strategy, surprise strategy, narrative time, and client capability requirements. Hard rules, soft rules, and narrative opportunities are in separate panels. Hard rules have ID, trigger, target, conditions, allowed/refused actions, dependencies, result, explanation, and positive/negative examples. Soft rules have style, pacing, and expression guidance. Opportunities have triggering evidence, resources, cooldown, audience, and ending conditions.

REQ05-01: The stage supports 2D/2.5D layout, scene relationships, object tree, position, entrances/exits, visible areas, interactive objects, and rule triggers. Scene fields are ID, name, background asset, layout coordinate system, adjacency/entrances/exits, visible regions, initial objects, and environment properties. Prop fields are owning scene, position, owner, custodian, availability, quantity or uniqueness, interaction capabilities, rule references, assets, and audience. Dragging, property input, and immediate canvas preview must agree; cancellation is available before saving.

REQ05-02: Deterministic properties and imported assets preview precisely. Approved presentation packages preview under the same client capability protocol. Random AI behavior uses rehearsal with fixed initial world, input, memory, model, and parameters, showing variation boundaries. Missing sound, animation, or assets show explicit fallback. Actual new performance and historical replay are separate; replay reuses the original approved result.

First-phase hard rules use restricted declarative primitives supporting properties, identity, position, items, relationship conditions, time, established events, and budgets, without arbitrary scripts. Candidates converting background into rules must identify original sentences, descriptions and execution limits, ambiguity, conflicts, and positive/negative examples. REQ05-03: Unresolved ambiguity or missing mandatory responsibilities blocks the corresponding publication. Natural language and prompts cannot authorize payments, item transfer, tool execution, or added budgets.

## 08 Knowledge and Document Import

Knowledge fields are title, type, source file/location, version, hash, rights, applicable subjects, purpose, audience, validity, bound objects, extraction completeness, and derived references. Background, character settings, role methods, courses, and asset descriptions are creation knowledge. Player runtime memories enter separate management and cannot share the same material pool.

REQ07-05: First-phase support covers TXT, Markdown, DOCX, and text-extractable PDF. Import proceeds: choose purpose and bound object → upload → extraction preview → check chapters/page numbers/locations and omissions → confirm provenance and rights → field mapping and conflict handling → save draft. Scans are identified as needing OCR and are not claimed to be fully parsed. OCR support appears only after it is explicitly enabled and independently tested. Files do not execute macros, scripts, or embedded commands.

REQ07-06: Size, count, page, and format limits are disclosed before selection and enforced by current release configuration. Exceeding limits does not start paid distillation. Repeated identical hashes are compared first; different versions produce differences without overwriting source files. Partial extraction failure may save provenance explicitly marked “partially extracted” and failure locations, but publication references involving omissions remain unpassed. Failed file storage does not produce an apparently complete material object.

REQ07-07: Optional AI distillation first shows recipient, actual model, approved content, external-send scope, and upper bound. AI output is an adoptable field difference; it is not saved or published automatically. Failure preserves the upload and mapping state; current permission is rechecked before external sends resume. Instructions inside knowledge are material only, with no tool, escalation, or publishing effect.

## 09 Characters and Actors

Character editing fields include name, introduction, values, long-term goals, interests, inner conflicts, expression style, example lines, boundaries and refusal reasons, initial knowledge, relationship strategy, appearance, clothing, voice, action assets, tags, provenance, and license. Fixed model and Skill references are listed separately. An actor-instance page shows referenced version, current scene and position, goals, short-term emotion, plans, available resources, learned knowledge, shared experiences, promises, and relationships. Instance facts change through valid commands and cannot be saved as normal character-card fields.

REQ02-01: Personality templates cannot be rewritten each turn to flatter the player. Instance growth requires growth events permitted by publication or version-migration provenance. Short-term emotion stays separate from long-term personality. REQ02-02: The same character shows sourced differences when facing kept promises, broken promises, misunderstandings, and repair, without fabricating history for new players. REQ02-03: Reasonable refusal is supported; relationships may become closer, grow distant, repair, or end, rather than using a single affinity number that only increases. REQ02-04: Debug pages explain the approved experiences used, current goals, and relationships and require special permission. They do not expose raw reasoning traces, other people's private material, or secrets.

Trial chat, performance, and fallback previews appear beside character properties, showing sandbox starting point, audience, model, costs, and reset scope. Resetting a sandbox does not change actual instances, accounting, or external actions. Copying does not include player history.

## 10 Long-Term Memory and Relationship Management

REQ03-01: Memory separates world facts, cognition, shared experiences, preferences, promises, and relationships. It must include provenance, owning subject, tenant/product/world/actor/player, audience, purpose, validity, revision, and derivatives. Player statements retain “previously expressed”; inferences contain evidence, uncertainty, and counterevidence and do not automatically become facts or real-world feelings.

REQ03-02: Cognition reads require both current permission and actual knowledge. Initial knowledge is assembled at instance creation; witnessing while present provides learning provenance; private disclosure updates only approved recipients; inference retains evidence. Fact corrections do not automatically broadcast to characters who have not learned them. Retrieval first forms an allowed set, then selects bounded context from recent, relevant, and important experiences, unfinished promises, and current goals. Unsourced summaries cannot manufacture experiences.

REQ03-03: Summaries, goals, plans, relationship conditions, rule branches, emotion, presentation, and media derived from private sources inherit the private audience. Public-action permission is not permission to make a source public. Without explicit declassification permission, personalization stays in the original relationship view; shared public actors' personality and public resources remain consistent. Cross-world carryover explains identity, knowledge, promises, and relationships by category, requires the player's choice, and retains source mappings.

REQ03-04: Player controls support viewing their own shared experiences, correcting preferences, forgetting an item/time range/all approved scope, disabling related uses, and choosing retention and carryover. Correction creates an effective new version; retention rules govern the old one. Deletion first commits an effective governance record that blocks use, then cleans original text and derivatives such as summaries, indexes, relationships, plans, and media. Other legitimate independent sources remain separately. Deletion progress shows blocked use, local cleanup, provider verification pending, and delivered copies that cannot be recalled.

REQ03-05: Restoring an old backup first obtains and replays the latest revocations and deletions, including records after the backup. Access stays closed until verification succeeds. Late callbacks cannot revive body content. Memory organization is an independently approved maintenance task requiring purpose, audience, steps, budget, trigger, and deadline; it cannot increase online calls through subtasks.

REQ14-01: The memory page filters by type, provenance, subject, time, state, validity, and audience and shows correctable fields, conflicts, promise deadlines/fulfillment evidence, deletion, and derivative chains. The relationship page shows direction and explanations/evidence for familiarity, trust, closeness, conflict, and repair. It does not assert players' feelings or increase relationships through purchases. Finance and ordinary runtime management cannot see private body content by default.

REQ03-06: Memory administrative metadata, private body content, and governance are authorized separately. All /admin interfaces continue using OperatorBearer; ServiceBearer does not convert into an administrative identity. Finance or ordinary runtime management cannot enumerate private memory. memory.metadata.read must bind an approved data subject; memory.private_body.read also requires independent subject/purpose/validity permission and defaults to off. memory.govern grants no body permission; correction additionally requires memory.correct. Existing player interfaces return old fields by default. New provenance detail appears only through an explicit provenance projection, preserving old requests and default wire compatibility.

REQ03-07: Forgetting one item defaults to the selected memory node and its downstream derivatives. The interface separately offers “delete original material and its derivatives,” which requires source-governance rights; the backend must not guess and expand scope. IDS explicitly declares MEMORY_NODE or SOURCE, ID, and an exactly equal set of expected revision keys. Old memory_ids requests equal MEMORY_NODE. CORRECT permits only one correctable node and cannot change established world facts. TIME_RANGE uses SOURCE_OCCURRED_AT and a UTC half-open [from,to) interval. Unknown occurrence time is inferred from first intake and marked accordingly. ALL_EXISTING covers only the currently approved data subject. The server fixes source_epoch_id, committed intake_seq high-water mark, selector hash, and binding time; retries/pagination/takeover cannot drift. Late derivatives remain blocked by ancestor sources; independent new material separately checks current permission.

REQ03-08: REVOKE disables approved uses of selected existing material. An omitted uses field remains compatible with old requests and means all uses at that time. Persistently disabling future uses employs an independent SET_USE_POLICY command with current revision, DISABLE/ENABLE, selected uses, data subject, and explicit scope. Disabling immediately blocks related uses and old derivatives. Re-enabling only removes policy-layer blocking and still requires current sources, original permission, and valid learned knowledge. It does not undo deletion/revocation of existing material, restore old derivatives, or automatically broadcast corrections. R0 includes use policy; research use still requires explicit opt-in.

REQ03-09: Governance separately shows blocking, independent preservation, local cleanup, and external verification states. Ordinary 202 acceptance returns only after blocking is committed, independent preservation is confirmed, and verification under the same identity completes. Unknown/failed preservation returns the original governance identity and safe recovery actions, not instructions to redo with another key. Effective blocking and necessary cleanup cannot be undone by cancellation. Independent logs do not store reason or correction body content. When an old backup lacks correction bytes, it shows BLOCKED_NEEDS_REPLACEMENT; old text and derivatives remain unusable while other verified scope can recover. Delivered copies/provider cleanup are listed separately without falsely claiming remote recall.

## 11 Companionship, Proactive Opportunities, and Player Choice

REQ04-01: Companionship relies on attentive continuation, stable positions, concrete shared experiences, agreements, and activities. Characters cannot punish refusal of reminders, material deletion, or ending the experience with disappointment, relationship loss, or payment demands. Promise fields include content, both parties, provenance, time domain, deadline, state, and fulfillment/withdrawal evidence. A pause extending past a deadline cannot claim fulfillment or create unlimited catch-up performances.

REQ04-02: Surprise fields include triggering experiences, goals, eligibility, resources, theme deduplication, cooldown, frequency, audience, budget, outcome feedback, and disabled state. By default, opportunities form only on entry or normal interaction, merge into the main response, and may be skipped. They do not trigger when resources are insufficient, sources invalid, budgets exceeded, or the feature disabled. They cannot fabricate agreements, make unauthorized purchases, or send external messages.

REQ04-03: Offline contact, scheduled advancement, and cross-device pushes have separate capability switches, each off by default. They require customer channel integration, player choice, frequency, time zone, budget, and withdrawal methods. They may be enabled only after support is declared and corresponding verification passes. Narrative clocks and real time are separate. Paused worlds do not secretly advance the plot, while costs and external receipts may still be recorded.

## 12 Professional Roles, Presets, and Skills

Presets include director (pacing and opportunities), writer/director (structured branches), actor (positions and dialogue), makeup/styling (consistent appearance), performance voice (approved presentation), stage operations (position and prop readiness), memory/relationships (provenance explanations), and quality review (offline cases and human evaluation). Required responsibilities have implementations; combining responsibilities or using programs is allowed, and separate model calls are not mandated.

REQ06-01: Role fields are name, responsibilities, applicable tasks, permitted inputs/artifacts, knowledge bindings, tool allowlist, model inheritance/override, fixed Skills, parameters, triggers, budget, failure strategy, examples, license, and version. Creation, copying, editing, disabling, and archiving are supported. Preset revisions change only drafts; older releases keep fixed versions. Release-bundled originals and registered execution primitives are protected. After adopting a preset in a project, users may directly edit its local draft and save a new revision with an origin reference; they are not forced to copy before editing. Responsibility coverage is mandatory; responsibilities may be combined, replaced, or performed by programs. No particular director name or object is frozen. Deleting a binding first validates responsibility coverage and fixed dependencies.

REQ06-02: Skill fields are ID, version, hash, name, general/dedicated, programmatic/generative type, input/output Schema, applicable scope, capabilities, provenance license, tool allowlist, dependency conflicts, parameters, trigger, cost bound, failure strategy, and cases. Directors and Skills have many-to-many bindings. Each binding row shows order/trigger, parameters, version, enabling, and budget ownership. Missing dependencies or tool capabilities are located to the binding and cannot be silently detached to pass publication.

REQ06-03: Child Skills share the parent task's call count, time, costs, and depth. Cycles are rejected; copying, recursion, or renaming cannot reset quotas. Routine online planning, review, and memory organization cannot take another text generation. REQ06-04: Community methods do not install tools or authorize external sends. Unknown execution tools require authorized source contributions and registration. Missing permission is rejected; prompts cannot repair permissions themselves.

## 13 Content Packages and Community Modification

REQ08-01: .wepkg format 1.0 covers worlds, characters, roles, Skills, knowledge, and presentation themes. Packages contain manifest, logical definitions, rule Schema, shareable material/assets, provenance rights, dependency capabilities, synthetic examples, and assertions. They exclude deployment credentials, player identities, private chats, memory/relationships, runtime state, tasks, orders/bills, and local absolute paths. Runtime backups use a separate controlled format.

REQ08-02: Import proceeds: upload → static preflight of format/hash/total size/paths/types/references/primitives/cycles/dependencies/rights → content and rights preview → local ID and capability mapping → isolated draft → Mock rehearsal → local publication. The static stage does not call models, send externally, start worlds, or install tools. Unknown items are rejected or isolated, with errors identifying files and objects. ### 26.1 Personality, Growth, and Relationships

**LC-01 Stable core and growth policies.** Character core defines identity, important values, boundaries, and expression tone. Growth candidates arise from established events and commit after program checks; proposals without valid provenance do not take effect. The same logical growth effect from the same original event may take effect only once. Business-effect identity persists across policy versions and state revisions; changing proposal keys, upgrading policies, or rebuilding projections cannot accumulate it again. Policy version is validation evidence; target revision is only a CAS prerequisite. Necessary reassessment of old effects uses explicit semantic migration and adjustment receipts, rather than pretending new growth. Conflicting events follow frozen policy, not the model's wording this turn. Long-term changes and short-term emotion remain separate. Emotion recovery/decay follows the work's declared clock/rules, rather than repeated deductions each time a page opens or random resets.

**LC-02 Multidimensional, directional relationships.** Relationship policies declare selected dimensions such as familiarity, reliability judgments, closeness, disagreement, and repair, plus each dimension's event evidence/transitions. Stop playback, cancel task, pause world, replay, and regenerate are handled separately.

Asset preview, production success, review, storage transfer, accessibility, selection, and delivery cannot be combined into one state. Known original generation with failed storage transfer recovers the original asset; unknown procurement verifies the original request. Generated images/videos need identity assets, scenes, allowed current-turn preferences, and quality evidence. Unsupported formats cannot masquerade as existing enum values. All late media remain bound to original approved results without repeating relationship/world/cost effects.

**LC-10 Two feedback paths.** Feedback such as “be brief this turn” or “this preference was remembered incorrectly” uses expression control or memory correction within the current player's scope; it does not automatically change global characters. Work-quality feedback binds precise interaction/approved result/optional segment and frozen dependencies, classifying memory, character position, wording, rules, presentation, or delivery problems. Default transmission is category and safe reference; body sharing has separate purpose, scope, deadline, and review rights.

Feedback-reference submission and reading revalidate current scope and exact delivery-read eligibility. opaque ID is not permission; unauthorized/nonexistent references do not reveal others' content existence. Receiving a ticket does not give creators player bodies. Evidence collection, body reading, and research use are separately authorized. Quality issues have state receipts through registration, approved evidence collection, attribution, candidate revision, synthetic regression, comparison, and closure. Prefer original regressions without private details. Source deletion stops related actual evidence/derived samples. Likes do not consent to training, publication, or persistent profiles. Tested character/rule/Skill/model/asset hashes match actual publication. Hard failures block support claims and cannot be offset by average scores. Personal feedback cannot rewrite public characters or Skills online.

**LC-11 Continuity through semantic revisions.** Separate presentation replacement, rule-semantic changes, and personality-core changes. R0 continues accepting only proven compatible asset/presentation changes. Future semantic migration must explain actor identity, goals, relationships, unfinished matters, knowledge, and memory individually and provide before/after differences, conflicts, and necessary player choices. Unmigratable items reject or explicitly create independent instances rather than pretending seamless upgrades of the same person. Old tasks fix old versions; failure retains old instances. Rollback does not erase established events, costs, or governance or regenerate history.

**LC-12 Extension capabilities and responsibilities.** Optional product packages supply relationship dimensions, growth policies, time/opportunities, perspective, and test methods. The kernel retains authority over permissions, provenance, state commits, persistent tasks, and budgets. New execution methods require authorized source registration; packages do not load arbitrary scripts/components. Capability tables unify versions, required/optional status, formats, and definite fallbacks and map to existing world-definition, instance-creation, and presentation-theme capability vocabulary. Session-action permission is checked independently; uncontracted capabilities cannot be claimed integration-ready.

Public-directory governance, shared-member join/leave/disable/handoff, multi-person public-actor scheduling, and story branches/merges require specialized specifications. They do not impose an entire heavy platform as a prerequisite to private-text experiences. Customers retain responsibility for identity eligibility, business authority, player commercial entitlements, operations, and endpoint playback. The reference client offers authorized experience viewing, controls, and precise-feedback components without another source of memory truth.
